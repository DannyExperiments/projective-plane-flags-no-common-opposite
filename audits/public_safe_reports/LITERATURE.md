# Literature and original question

**Search date** 26 November 2026.

## Source question

Sira Busch and Hendrik Van Maldeghem ask whether the cyclic flags of a triangle in the plane of order two are the sole sets of size s+1 with no common opposite whose point entries do not form a full line and whose line entries do not form a full pencil. The conjecture occurs in Remark 5.4 in both arXiv versions. In v1 the remark points back to Claim 4 of Proposition 5.2, and in v2 it points back to Claim 5. The author-hosted 50-page July revision has Remark 5.4 on PDF page 45. The article appeared online in *Annals of Combinatorics* on 31 July 2026. The publisher preview supplies the version-of-record citation, and the full typeset text requires subscription access. The remark numbering here identifies the preprints and author-hosted copy. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00026-026-00842-w) · [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01110v1#S5.SS1) · [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01110v2#S5.SS1) · [Author-hosted copy](https://cage.ugent.be/~hvm/artikels/ExceptionalLines.pdf).

Busch states the conjecture in Remark 9.9.0.4, printed p. 308, of *Groups of Automorphisms of Spherical Buildings*, University of Münster, December 2025, defended 29 January 2026. The thesis and preprints present it as a question after discussing a thin generalized hexagon. The question itself is about every finite projective plane. The manuscript proves that point and line entries are necessarily distinct for a qualifying set under the literal full line and pencil exclusions. [Thesis](https://sirabusch.de/dissertationSiraBusch.pdf#page=308) · [Thesis DOI](https://doi.org/10.17879/10908475556) · [University defense notice](https://www.uni-muenster.de/FB10/Service/show_article.shtml?brettid=&id=10856) · [Pinned catalog Q124](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/mathematics/geometry-differential-geometry-part-1.md#q124).

## Classical results

- A. Blokhuis and H. A. Wilbrink, “A characterization of exterior lines of certain sets of points in PG(2,q),” *Geometriae Dedicata* 23(2), 253–254 (1987), gives the nucleus bound used here. An affine set of q+1 points in AG(2,q) has at most q−1 nuclei. The bound is stated explicitly in A. Blokhuis and F. Mazzocca, “Lifts of nuclei in finite projective spaces,” Result 1.2, in *Finite Geometries and Combinatorics*, pp. 31–36 (1993). A line disjoint from the given point set supplies the chart. [Blokhuis–Wilbrink](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00181280) · [Blokhuis–Mazzocca](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511526336.005).
- A. Blokhuis, “On multiple nuclei and a conjecture of Lunelli and Sce,” *Bulletin of the Belgian Mathematical Society – Simon Stevin* 1(3), 349–353 (1994), introduction, records the classical extremal point configuration used in the construction and the sporadic Desargues-configuration example in order five. G. Korchmáros and F. Mazzocca classify pairs consisting of a point set of size q+1 on the union of two lines in PG(2,q) and its set of nuclei in *Combinatorica* 14(1), 63–69 (1994). The point entries here fall within that class. Their earlier note treats non-Desarguesian planes with additional collineation hypotheses. The present flag classification needs the line-union equality structure, both projections, and their compatible incidence matching. [Blokhuis article](https://www.maths.tcd.ie/EMIS/journals/BBMS/Bulletin/bul943/AART.PDF) · [Korchmáros–Mazzocca, Combinatorica](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01305951) · [Their earlier note](https://korandi.org/docs/misc/setsgraphsnumbers/setsgraphsnumbers31.pdf).
- A. A. Bruen, “Nuclei of sets of q+1 points in PG(2,q) and blocking sets of Rédei type,” *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A* 55(1), 130–132 (1990), concerns nuclei and point blocking sets and cites the general-plane work of Cameron and Fisher. The axiomatic construction herein is separate from the Desarguesian scope of the classification. [Bruen](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(90)90051-W).

## Nearby notions of opposition

- Busch and Van Maldeghem, *Discrete Mathematics* 349(2), 114711 (2026), Theorem A, classify sets of same-type vertices in classical spherical buildings that have no common opposite vertex. Q124 concerns chambers consisting of two incident vertex types. [Classical-type article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2025.114711) · [Author PDF](https://cage.ugent.be/~hvm/artikels/282.pdf).
- Heering, Metsch, Taranchuk and Weiner, *Polynesian Journal of Mathematics* 3(2), 1–8 (2026), study coloring the chamber opposition graph via pairwise non-opposition and incidence-free point–line pairs. Q124 asks for a set of vertices with empty common neighborhood. For example, the third and fourth flags of the manuscript's order-three witness are opposite to each other, so this witness is outside their coclique class. [Heering et al.](https://doi.org/10.69763/polyjmath.3.2) · [Open preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12030v3).
- Abramenko and Van Maldeghem, *Annals of Combinatorics* 4, 125–137 (2000), Remark 3.3, treat six-chamber configurations in projective planes under an opposite-count condition for every chamber, motivated by characterizing apartment chamber sets. Héger and Nagy study domination of the point–line incidence graph, where a point outside a selected set must be incident with a selected line. Here the example has uncovered points and relies on every line through each of them meeting a selected point. [Abramenko–Van Maldeghem](https://cage.ugent.be/~hvm/artikels/90.pdf) · [Héger–Nagy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.02933).

No earlier statement of the exact flag construction as a counterexample or the Desarguesian classification and count was located in these sources or in targeted searches for common opposition of flags/chambers, thin generalized hexagons, nucleus constructions, and citations of the source paper. This is a bounded literature check. The manuscript attributes the classical geometry and makes no priority assertion.
